How strong is a team, and how sure are we?

SGMT 431 · Lecture 5 · Overview

A 12-5 record answers neither question well.

  • it ignores margins;
  • it ignores who the opponents were; and
  • over 17 games it contains a great deal of luck.

Two teams finish 12-5 in 2023. One outscored opponents by 194 points, the other by 66. Are they equally strong?

One latent quantity, many observable outcomes

SGMT 431 · Lecture 5 · Overview

Let θj\theta_j represent team jj’s latent strength. For margin models it is measured in points; for Bradley-Terry it is measured in log-odds. Teams ii and jj are compared through θi−θj\theta_i-\theta_j. Poisson goal models use separate attack and defense effects:

winner,margin,or a pair of goal counts. \text{winner},\qquad\text{margin},\qquad\text{or a pair of goal counts}.

Every rating system is a rule for turning outcomes into estimates of θ\theta, and every one must handle noise, schedules, and home advantage.

A Gaussian prior on the ratings

SGMT 431 · Lecture 5.2 · Ratings from Margins: Least Squares, Ridge, and a Gaussian Prior

The Bradley-Terry model

P(i beats j)=πiπi+πj=logit⁡−1(θi−θj),θj=log⁡πj. P(i\text{ beats }j)=\frac{\pi_i}{\pi_i+\pi_j}=\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\theta_i-\theta_j), \qquad \theta_j=\log\pi_j.

P(home wins g)=logit⁡−1(αhg+∑jxgjθj): P(\text{home wins }g)=\operatorname{logit}^{-1}\!\left(\alpha h_g+\textstyle\sum_jx_{gj}\theta_j\right):

Elo is an online Bradley-Terry model

SGMT 431 · Lecture 5.3 · Win-Loss Ratings: Bradley-Terry and Logistic Models

The Elo system computes the expected score of team i against team j as

Ei=11+10−(Ri−Rj)/400=logit⁡−1(ln⁡10400(Ri−Rj))⇒θj=ln⁡10400Rj, E_i=\frac{1}{1+10^{-(R_i-R_j)/400}}=\operatorname{logit}^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\ln10}{400}(R_i-R_j)\right) \ \Longrightarrow\ \theta_j=\frac{\ln 10}{400}R_j,

since 10−x/400=e−xln⁡10/40010^{-x/400}=e^{-x\ln10/400}.

The gradient of one game’s log-likelihood is

∂∂θi[ylogp+(1−y)log(1−p)]=y−p, \frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_i}\left[y\log p+(1-y)\log(1-p)\right]=y-p,

Let c=ln⁡(10)/400c=\ln(10)/400. The Elo update Ri←Ri+K(Si−Ei)R_i\leftarrow R_i+K(S_i-E_i) implies θi←θi+cK(Si−Ei)\theta_i\leftarrow\theta_i+cK(S_i-E_i): a likelihood-gradient step of size cKcK on the log-odds scale for decisive games.

Ri,RjR_i,R_j: Elo ratings; Ei=pE_i=p: predicted win probability for team ii; Si=y∈{0,1}S_i=y\in\{0,1\}: observed win indicator. KK: update factor in Elo points; 400: conventional Elo scaling constant. θ\theta: log-odds rating. The displayed likelihood is Bernoulli; assigning 0.5 for a draw is an additional Elo convention.

Limitations and extensions

SGMT 431 · Lecture 5.4 · Goals as Counts: A Bayesian Multilevel Poisson Model
  • Independence: Dixon-Coles adjust the four lowest scorelines; bivariate Poisson adds a shared component.
  • Overdispersion: rare for goals, common for shots and corners; negative binomial.
  • Time: exponential down-weighting of old matches, or an explicit dynamic model (Section 5.5).
  • Expected goals as the response: less noise, different target.
