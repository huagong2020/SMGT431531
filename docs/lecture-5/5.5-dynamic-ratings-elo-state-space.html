5.5 Dynamic Ratings: Elo and State-Space Models

Author

Hua Gong

1 Learning objectives

After this tutorial, you should be able to:

  • implement the Elo system from scratch and interpret the update constant, home advantage, and between-season regression;
  • tune Elo by the quality of its pregame forecasts;
  • write a state-space model for team strength and explain the role of each variance;
  • derive the Kalman filter’s predict and update steps and show that Elo is a constant-gain version of it;
  • read the Stan implementation of the state-space model and interpret its posterior; and
  • distinguish filtered from smoothed estimates.

2 Why static ratings are not enough

Every model so far assigns one rating per team per season and treats week 1 and week 18 as equally informative about the team today. Strength changes: quarterbacks get hurt, rosters turn over, and between seasons the changes are larger still. A rating that is supposed to describe a team now should weight recent games more heavily, and it should carry uncertainty that grows when the team has not played.

3 The Elo system

Elo maintains a rating RjR_j for each team and updates it after every game. For a game between home team ii and away team jj with home advantage AA rating points,

Ei=11+10−(Ri+A−Rj)/400,Ri←Ri+K(Si−Ei),Rj←Rj−K(Si−Ei), E_i=\frac{1}{1+10^{-(R_i+A-R_j)/400}}, \qquad R_i\leftarrow R_i+K(S_i-E_i), \qquad R_j\leftarrow R_j-K(S_i-E_i),

where SiS_i is 1 for a home win, 0 for a loss, and 0.5 for a tie. Tutorial 5.3 showed that EiE_i is a Bradley-Terry probability and that the update is a gradient step of size KK. Two further conventions are common.

  • Season regression. Before each new season, every rating is moved a fraction rr of the way back toward the starting value: Rj←Rj+r(1500−Rj)R_j\leftarrow R_j+r(1500-R_j). This encodes the belief that rosters change.
  • Margin of victory. A multiplier on KK that grows with the margin, with a correction so that heavy favorites do not gain rating by winning big, lets Elo use the score rather than only the result.

3.1 Tuning by forecast quality

The constant KK trades responsiveness against noise. Too small, and the ratings lag; too large, and they chase single results. The right way to choose it is by the quality of the pregame forecasts it produces. The 2022 season is treated as a warm-up so that ratings have separated before scoring begins.

score_elo <- function(k, home_advantage = 55, margin_multiplier = FALSE) {
  run <- run_elo(nfl, k = k, home_advantage = home_advantage, margin_multiplier = margin_multiplier)
  scored <- run$games %>% filter(season >= 2023, tie == 0)
  data.frame(
    k = k,
    home_advantage = home_advantage,

    log_loss = log_loss(scored$home_win, scored$home_prob),
    brier = brier_score(scored$home_win, scored$home_prob)
  )
}

4 A state-space model for strength

Elo’s update is a rule; a state-space model is a probability model whose optimal update we can derive. Following Glickman and Stern (1998), let θj,t\theta_{j,t} be team jj’s strength in period tt (one NFL week) and let strengths evolve as a random walk:

θj,t=θj,t−1+ωj,t,ωj,t∼N(0,σw2). \theta_{j,t}=\theta_{j,t-1}+\omega_{j,t}, \qquad \omega_{j,t}\sim N(0,\sigma_w^2).

Between seasons the change is larger and includes regression toward the mean:

θj,first week=ρθj,last week+vj,vj∼N(0,σs2),0<ρ<1. \theta_{j,\text{first week}}=\rho\,\theta_{j,\text{last week}}+v_j, \qquad v_j\sim N(0,\sigma_s^2), \qquad 0<\rho<1.

Each game observes a noisy difference of current strengths:

yg=θhome(g),t−θaway(g),t+h⋅homeg+εg,εg∼N(0,σ2). y_g=\theta_{\text{home}(g),t}-\theta_{\text{away}(g),t}+h\cdot\text{home}_g+\varepsilon_g, \qquad \varepsilon_g\sim N(0,\sigma^2).

The model has four variances and two constants: σ2\sigma^2 (game noise), σw2\sigma_w^2 (weekly drift), σs2\sigma_s^2 (between-season change), the initial spread, ρ\rho, and hh. Each has a direct meaning, which is the advantage over Elo’s KK.

4.1 The Kalman filter

Because every distribution is normal and every relationship is linear, the posterior of the strength vector 𝜽t\boldsymbol\theta_t given all games through period tt is normal, N(𝒎t,Pt)N(\mathbf m_t,P_t), and it can be updated in closed form. Write 𝑯g\mathbf H_g for the vector with +1+1 at the home team, −1-1 at the away team, and zeros elsewhere.

Predict (move to the next period):

𝒎t−=𝒎t−1,Pt−=Pt−1+σw2I. \mathbf m_t^{-}=\mathbf m_{t-1}, \qquad P_t^{-}=P_{t-1}+\sigma_w^2 I.

Update (observe game gg):

ŷg=𝑯g𝖳𝒎+h⋅homeg,Sg=𝑯g𝖳P𝑯g+σ2,𝒌g=P𝑯gSg, \hat y_g=\mathbf H_g^{\mathsf T}\mathbf m+h\cdot\text{home}_g, \qquad S_g=\mathbf H_g^{\mathsf T}P\mathbf H_g+\sigma^2, \qquad \mathbf k_g=\frac{P\mathbf H_g}{S_g},

𝒎←𝒎+𝒌g(yg−ŷg),P←P−𝒌g𝑯g𝖳P. \mathbf m\leftarrow\mathbf m+\mathbf k_g\left(y_g-\hat y_g\right), \qquad P\leftarrow P-\mathbf k_g\mathbf H_g^{\mathsf T}P.

The update is prediction error times a gain 𝒌g\mathbf k_g. The gain is large when the current uncertainty PP is large relative to the game noise σ2\sigma^2 and small when the ratings are already well established. This is Bayesian sequential updating: the posterior after each game is the prior for the next, exactly as in the beta-binomial example of Lecture 4, but with the drift step adding uncertainty between periods.

NoteShow derivation: Kalman prediction and update

The filter repeats two steps: predict the current strength distribution, then update it using the new result. We derive both below, following the scalar-to-vector approach in the accompanying study notes.

4.1.1 1. Prediction: carry the previous posterior forward

Let 𝒟t−1\mathcal D_{t-1} denote all results through the previous week. Suppose the strength vector after those results has distribution

𝜽t−1∣𝒟t−1∼N(𝒎t−1,Pt−1). \boldsymbol\theta_{t-1}\mid\mathcal D_{t-1} \sim N(\mathbf m_{t-1},P_{t-1}).

Here 𝒎t−1\mathbf m_{t-1} contains the estimated team strengths, and Pt−1P_{t-1} contains their variances and covariances. Start the first week with a normal prior, such as N(𝟎,σinitial2I)N(\mathbf0,\sigma_{\mathrm{initial}}^2I). For this derivation, treat the home advantage and noise variances as fixed. Assume the initial state, state innovations, and game errors are mutually independent, with independent noise across periods and games.

The weekly state equation is

𝜽t=𝜽t−1+𝝎t,𝝎t∼N(𝟎,σw2I). \boldsymbol\theta_t=\boldsymbol\theta_{t-1}+\boldsymbol\omega_t, \qquad \boldsymbol\omega_t\sim N(\mathbf0,\sigma_w^2I).

Take the conditional expectation of both sides. The new innovation has mean zero and is independent of the previous results, so

𝒎t−=E[𝜽t∣𝒟t−1]=E[𝜽t−1∣𝒟t−1]+E[𝝎t∣𝒟t−1]=𝒎t−1. \begin{aligned} \mathbf m_t^- &=E[\boldsymbol\theta_t\mid\mathcal D_{t-1}]\\ &=E[\boldsymbol\theta_{t-1}\mid\mathcal D_{t-1}] +E[\boldsymbol\omega_t\mid\mathcal D_{t-1}]\\ &=\boxed{\mathbf m_{t-1}}. \end{aligned}

Independence also makes the cross-covariance terms zero. Therefore

Pt−=Var⁡(𝜽t∣𝒟t−1)=Var⁡(𝜽t−1∣𝒟t−1)+Var⁡(𝝎t)=Pt−1+σw2I. \begin{aligned} P_t^-&=\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol\theta_t\mid\mathcal D_{t-1})\\ &=\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol\theta_{t-1}\mid\mathcal D_{t-1}) +\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol\omega_t)\\ &=\boxed{P_{t-1}+\sigma_w^2I}. \end{aligned}

A sum of independent normal vectors is normal, giving 𝜽t∣𝒟t−1∼N(𝒎t−,Pt−)\boldsymbol\theta_t\mid\mathcal D_{t-1}\sim N(\mathbf m_t^-,P_t^-). The minus sign means before the current observations. Time leaves expected strength unchanged but increases uncertainty. This is prediction through the state equation, not yet a Bayes update.

At a season boundary the equation is instead 𝜽new=ρ𝜽old+𝝂\boldsymbol\theta_{\mathrm{new}}=\rho\boldsymbol\theta_{\mathrm{old}}+ \boldsymbol\nu, with independent 𝝂∼N(𝟎,σs2I)\boldsymbol\nu\sim N(\mathbf0,\sigma_s^2I). The same reasoning gives

𝒎new−=ρ𝒎old,Pnew−=ρ2Pold+σs2I. \boxed{\mathbf m_{\mathrm{new}}^-=\rho\mathbf m_{\mathrm{old}}}, \qquad \boxed{P_{\mathrm{new}}^-=\rho^2P_{\mathrm{old}}+\sigma_s^2I}.

The mean is multiplied by ρ\rho, while the covariance is multiplied by ρ2\rho^2, because variances scale quadratically.

4.1.2 2. Update in one dimension: multiply the prior by the likelihood

First consider just one unknown strength and one measurement:

θ∣𝒟∼N(m,P),Y∣θ,𝒟∼N(θ,r),P,r>0. \theta\mid\mathcal D\sim N(m,P),\qquad Y\mid\theta,\mathcal D\sim N(\theta,r),\qquad P,r>0.

Here 𝒟\mathcal D is the information already incorporated, PP is the prior variance, and rr is the measurement-error variance. Bayes’ rule is

p(θ∣y,𝒟)=p(y∣θ,𝒟)p(θ∣𝒟)p(y∣𝒟)∝p(y∣θ,𝒟)p(θ∣𝒟). p(\theta\mid y,\mathcal D) =\frac{p(y\mid\theta,\mathcal D)p(\theta\mid\mathcal D)} {p(y\mid\mathcal D)} \propto p(y\mid\theta,\mathcal D)p(\theta\mid\mathcal D).

The denominator does not depend on the candidate θ\theta, so it can be absorbed into a normalizing constant. Substituting the two normal densities,

p(θ∣y,𝒟)∝exp⁡[−12{(θ−m)2P+(y−θ)2r}]. p(\theta\mid y,\mathcal D)\propto \exp\left[-\frac12\left\{ \frac{(\theta-m)^2}{P}+\frac{(y-\theta)^2}{r}\right\}\right].

Expand the expression in braces and collect powers of θ\theta:

(1P+1r)θ2−2(mP+yr)θ+C, \left(\frac1P+\frac1r\right)\theta^2 -2\left(\frac mP+\frac yr\right)\theta+C,

where CC does not depend on θ\theta. Define

P+=(1P+1r)−1=PrP+r,m+=P+(mP+yr)=rm+PyP+r. P^+=\left(\frac1P+\frac1r\right)^{-1}=\frac{Pr}{P+r}, \qquad m^+=P^+\left(\frac mP+\frac yr\right) =\frac{rm+Py}{P+r}.

Completing the square gives (θ−m+)2/P++C′(\theta-m^+)^2/P^++C'. Thus the posterior is N(m+,P+)N(m^+,P^+). Set K=P/(P+r)K=P/(P+r) and rearrange:

m+=m+K(y−m),P+=(1−K)P. \boxed{m^+=m+K(y-m)},\qquad \boxed{P^+=(1-K)P}.

The posterior mean is (1−K)m+Ky(1-K)m+Ky: a weighted average of the prior mean and the observation. Larger prior uncertainty gives more weight to the new result; larger measurement noise gives less. A normal prior and likelihood together, not normal observation noise alone, produce the normal posterior. This is why prediction and updating preserve normality when we start from a normal initial state.

4.1.3 3. Update for teams: a game measures a strength difference

Immediately before game gg, write 𝜽∣𝒟∼N(𝒎,P)\boldsymbol\theta\mid\mathcal D\sim N(\mathbf m,P). These are the current values, including any games already processed this week. Let HgH_g have +1 for the home team, -1 for the away team, and zero elsewhere:

Yg=HgT𝜽+hhomeg+εg,εg∼N(0,σ2). Y_g=H_g^T\boldsymbol\theta+h\,\mathrm{home}_g+\varepsilon_g, \qquad \varepsilon_g\sim N(0,\sigma^2).

With the venue and model parameters fixed, taking expectation and variance gives the predicted margin and its variance:

ŷg=E[Yg∣𝒟]=HgT𝒎+hhomeg, \hat y_g=E[Y_g\mid\mathcal D] =H_g^T\mathbf m+h\,\mathrm{home}_g, Sg=Var⁡(Yg∣𝒟)=HgTPHg+σ2. S_g=\operatorname{Var}(Y_g\mid\mathcal D) =H_g^TPH_g+\sigma^2.

The strength–observation covariance follows from independence of the observation error:

Cov⁡(𝜽,Yg∣𝒟)=Cov⁡(𝜽,HgT𝜽+εg∣𝒟)=PHg. \operatorname{Cov}(\boldsymbol\theta,Y_g\mid\mathcal D) =\operatorname{Cov}(\boldsymbol\theta,H_g^T\boldsymbol\theta+ \varepsilon_g\mid\mathcal D)=PH_g.

The state and observation are jointly normal, with

(𝜽Yg)∣𝒟∼N((𝒎ŷg),(PPHgHgTPSg)). \begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol\theta\\Y_g\end{pmatrix}\mid\mathcal D \sim N\left( \begin{pmatrix}\mathbf m\\\hat y_g\end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix}P&PH_g\\H_g^TP&S_g\end{pmatrix} \right).

The vector version of completing the square uses the conditional-normal identities

E[X∣Z=z]=μX+ΣXZΣZZ−1(z−μZ), E[X\mid Z=z]=\mu_X+\Sigma_{XZ}\Sigma_{ZZ}^{-1}(z-\mu_Z), Var⁡(X∣Z=z)=ΣXX−ΣXZΣZZ−1ΣZX. \operatorname{Var}(X\mid Z=z) =\Sigma_{XX}-\Sigma_{XZ}\Sigma_{ZZ}^{-1}\Sigma_{ZX}.

Substitute X=𝜽X=\boldsymbol\theta, Z=YgZ=Y_g, ΣXZ=PHg\Sigma_{XZ}=PH_g, and ΣZZ=Sg\Sigma_{ZZ}=S_g. Because SgS_g is a scalar, its inverse is simply 1/Sg1/S_g. We obtain

𝒌g=PHgSg,𝒎+=𝒎+𝒌g(yg−ŷg), \boxed{\mathbf k_g=\frac{PH_g}{S_g}},\qquad \boxed{\mathbf m^+=\mathbf m+\mathbf k_g(y_g-\hat y_g)}, P+=P−PHgHgTPSg=P−𝒌gHgTP. \boxed{P^+=P-\frac{PH_gH_g^TP}{S_g} =P-\mathbf k_gH_g^TP}.

These are the lecture’s update formulas: prediction error multiplied by a gain, followed by a reduction in uncertainty. All right-hand sides use the values before the update. In particular, if the home and away teams are AA and BB,

HgTPHg=PAA+PBB−2PAB,kg,j=PjA−PjBPAA+PBB−2PAB+σ2. H_g^TPH_g=P_{AA}+P_{BB}-2P_{AB},\qquad k_{g,j}=\frac{P_{jA}-P_{jB}} {P_{AA}+P_{BB}-2P_{AB}+\sigma^2}.

The covariance term matters: team strengths need not remain independent once game results have been incorporated.

4.1.4 4. A numerical example

Use the values from the study notes:

𝒎=(4−1),P=(90016),Hg=(1−1),h=3,homeg=1,σ2=100. \mathbf m=\begin{pmatrix}4\\-1\end{pmatrix},\quad P=\begin{pmatrix}9&0\\0&16\end{pmatrix},\quad H_g=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix},\quad h=3,\quad\mathrm{home}_g=1,\quad\sigma^2=100.

The predicted margin is ŷg=4−(−1)+3=8\hat y_g=4-(-1)+3=8. Suppose the home team wins by 18, so the prediction error is 10. Then

Sg=9+16+100=125,𝒌g=1125(9−16)=(0.072−0.128), S_g=9+16+100=125,\qquad \mathbf k_g=\frac1{125}\begin{pmatrix}9\\-16\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}0.072\\-0.128\end{pmatrix}, 𝒎+=(4−1)+(0.072−0.128)10=(4.72−2.28), \mathbf m^+=\begin{pmatrix}4\\-1\end{pmatrix} +\begin{pmatrix}0.072\\-0.128\end{pmatrix}10 =\begin{pmatrix}4.72\\-2.28\end{pmatrix}, P+=(90016)−1125(81−144−144256)=(8.3521.1521.15213.952). P^+=\begin{pmatrix}9&0\\0&16\end{pmatrix} -\frac1{125}\begin{pmatrix}81&-144\\-144&256\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}8.352&1.152\\1.152&13.952\end{pmatrix}.

The away team moves more because its prior variance was larger. Both individual variances decrease, and the variance of the strength difference falls from 25 to 8.352+13.952−2(1.152)=208.352+13.952-2(1.152)=20.

For several games in the same week, use each updated distribution as the prior for the next game. Add weekly drift only when moving to a new week, not after every game. The recurring sequence is:

Previous posterior → predict through the state equation → current prior → observe a game and apply Bayes’ rule → current posterior.

NoteElo as a constant-gain filter

Elo’s update K(Si−Ei)K(S_i-E_i) has the same form, prediction error times a gain, with a constant gain KK and a binary observation. In the state-space model the gain settles to a steady value once PP stops changing, which happens when the uncertainty added by drift each week balances the uncertainty removed by each game. Elo with a well-chosen KK approximates that steady-state filter. The state-space model adds three things: a gain that starts large for new teams, an explicit uncertainty for every rating, and hyperparameters that can be estimated rather than tuned.

5 The Stan implementation

The Kalman filter needs the hyperparameters. The Stan program below estimates them jointly with every team-week strength by sampling from the full posterior.

// Dynamic team ratings: a random-walk state-space model for game margins
// in the spirit of Glickman and Stern (1998).
//
// theta[j, t] is the strength of team j in period t (one period per NFL week).
// Within a season the strengths follow a random walk; between seasons they
// are shrunk toward zero by a factor rho and receive a larger innovation.
//
// margin_i ~ normal(theta[home_i, t_i] - theta[away_i, t_i] + h * home_i, sigma_obs)

data {
  int<lower=1> N;                             // games
  int<lower=1> J;                             // teams
  int<lower=1> T;                             // periods (weeks across seasons)
  array[N] int<lower=1, upper=J> home;
  array[N] int<lower=1, upper=J> away;
  array[N] int<lower=1, upper=T> period;
  vector[N] margin;
  vector<lower=0, upper=1>[N] home_field;     // 0 at neutral sites
  array[T] int<lower=0, upper=1> season_start; // 1 if period t starts a new season
}

parameters {
  matrix[J, T] z;                              // standardized innovations
  real<lower=0> sigma_obs;
  real<lower=0> sigma_week;
  real<lower=0> sigma_season;
  real<lower=0> sigma_initial;
  real<lower=0, upper=1> rho;
  real h;
}

transformed parameters {
  matrix[J, T] theta;
  theta[, 1] = sigma_initial * z[, 1];
  for (t in 2:T) {
    if (season_start[t] == 1) {
      theta[, t] = rho * theta[, t - 1] + sigma_season * z[, t];
    } else {
      theta[, t] = theta[, t - 1] + sigma_week * z[, t];
    }
  }
}

model {
  to_vector(z) ~ std_normal();
  sigma_obs ~ normal(0, 20);
  sigma_week ~ normal(0, 3);
  sigma_season ~ normal(0, 6);
  sigma_initial ~ normal(0, 10);
  rho ~ beta(4, 2);
  h ~ normal(2, 3);

  {
    vector[N] mu;
    for (i in 1:N) {
      mu[i] = theta[home[i], period[i]] - theta[away[i], period[i]] + h * home_field[i];
    }
    margin ~ normal(mu, sigma_obs);
  }
}

generated quantities {
  vector[N] log_lik;
  for (i in 1:N) {
    real mu_i = theta[home[i], period[i]] - theta[away[i], period[i]] + h * home_field[i];
    log_lik[i] = normal_lpdf(margin[i] | mu_i, sigma_obs);
  }
}

The transformed parameters block builds the strength paths from standardized innovations, a non-centered parameterization that keeps the geometry friendly for Hamiltonian Monte Carlo. The generated quantities block stores each game’s log-likelihood for model comparison.

The hyperparameter posteriors above tell a coherent story.

  • σ\sigma (sigma_obs) is about 12 points: the irreducible noise in one NFL margin.
  • σw\sigma_w (sigma_week) is well under one point per week, so within-season drift over 17 weeks has a standard deviation of only a few points.
  • σs\sigma_s (sigma_season) is about four points and ρ\rho about 0.65: between seasons, ratings regress about a third of the way toward zero and then receive a shock comparable to the whole within-season drift. The Elo rule of thumb of one-third regression is close to what the data say.
  • hh is about two points of home advantage.

5.1 Filtered against smoothed

The Stan posterior for θj,t\theta_{j,t} uses every game, including those played after week tt; that is a smoothed estimate. A filtered estimate after week tt uses games through that week. The helper’s plotted filtered table is saved before each week’s games, so it contains predicted states based on earlier games, conditional on the supplied hyperparameters. Filtering and smoothing agree after the final game for the same fixed hyperparameters; the Stan results also average over hyperparameter uncertainty.

7 Key takeaways

  • Elo is a constant-gain sequential update whose KK should be tuned by forecast quality; a margin multiplier improves it.
  • A random-walk state-space model gives every rating a time index and an uncertainty, with hyperparameters that mean something.
  • The Kalman filter is the exact Bayesian update for that model and reduces to an Elo-like rule with a data-driven gain.
  • Stan estimates the hyperparameters and yields smoothed paths; valid historical forecasts require filtering with hyperparameters chosen without future outcomes.
