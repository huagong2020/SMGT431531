Lecture 4: Player Performance Evaluation

Author

Hua Gong

1 The question for this lecture

How much of a player’s measured performance is the player, and how much is the situation, the teammates, the opponents, and chance?

Every box-score statistic is an average over a limited number of plays. A quarterback with a 72% completion rate over 40 attempts and a quarterback with a 68% rate over 560 attempts are not equally well measured, and neither number separates the passer from the receivers who caught the ball or the defenses that allowed it. This lecture builds a set of tools for that problem:

  1. Multilevel models treat each player as one group in a population of players and estimate how much of the observed variation is signal. The result is partial pooling: every player’s estimate is pulled toward the league average by an amount that depends on how much we know about that player.
  2. Bayesian inference makes the same idea explicit. A prior distribution describes the population, the data update it, and the posterior describes what we believe about each player and how uncertain we are.
  3. The connection is partial pooling through a population distribution. ML or REML estimates population parameters and gives group estimates with an empirical Bayes interpretation. A fully Bayesian model assigns priors to all unknown parameters and integrates their uncertainty. Gaussian conditional means coincide with mixed-model predictions under matched assumptions, but a logistic mixed-model mode need not equal a posterior mean.

3 Data

Two public sources are stored locally so the tutorials render without an internet connection.

NFL pass attempts. The file data/nfl_passes_2023.rds holds every 2023 regular-season pass attempt from nflverse play-by-play data after removing sacks, spikes, scrambles, and two-point attempts and keeping only plays with a recorded air-yards value, pass location, passer, and receiver.

library(dplyr)
source("R/player_helpers.R")

passes <- load_passes()

data.frame(
  attempts = nrow(passes),
  passers = n_distinct(passes$passer),
  receivers = n_distinct(passes$receiver),
  defenses = n_distinct(passes$defteam),
  completion_rate = round(mean(passes$complete), 3)
) %>%
  knitr::kable()
attempts passers receivers defenses completion_rate
17483 102 482 32 0.675

WNBA shooting. The file data/wnba_2024.rds holds Caitlin Clark’s 2024 regular-season game log and the season three-point totals of every WNBA player with at least 40 attempts, from ESPN box scores through wehoop.

data/README.md records the provenance and filters for both files.

NoteScope of the examples

The pass-level data contain no pressure, coverage, or route information beyond what nflverse provides, so the models attribute anything they cannot see to the passer, the receiver, the defense, or noise. Treat every rating in this lecture as a demonstration of a method, not as a finished evaluation of any player.
