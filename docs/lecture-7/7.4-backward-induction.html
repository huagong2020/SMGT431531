7.4 Backward Induction on Fourth Down

Author

Hua Gong

1 Learning objectives

  • Write the full state tuples at t=2t=2 and t=1t=1.
  • Apply the Bellman optimality equation in two steps, first at t=1t=1 and then at t=2t=2.
  • Measure how a restriction on future play changes the current call.

2 Full state tuples and the game timeline

Let tt count the scrimmage plays remaining. Play proceeds from fourth down at t=2t=2, to the final snap at t=1t=1, to the game result at t=0t=0. Backward induction solves the problem in the opposite order.

Use the same six fields for every live state:

s=(Possession,Down,Yards to go,Yard line,Score differential,t). s=(\text{Possession},\ \text{Down},\ \text{Yards to go},\ \text{Yard line},\ \text{Score differential},\ t).

The yard line is relative to the team in possession. The score differential is always our score minus the opponent’s score, even when the opponent has the ball.

2.1 Two plays left (t=2t=2)

Our team has the ball and trails 20–22. The only starting state is 𝒮2={s0}\mathcal S_2=\{s_0\}, where

s0=(Us,4th,2,Opp. 40,−2,2). s_0=(\text{Us},\ 4\text{th},\ 2,\ \text{Opp. 40},\ -2,\ 2).

The subscript in s0s_0 labels the starting state. Its last field gives the number of plays left.

2.2 One play left (t=1t=1)

The reachable state space is 𝒮1={slead,strail,s1st}\mathcal S_1=\{s_{\mathrm{lead}},s_{\mathrm{trail}},s_{\mathrm{1st}}\}. In each of these states, the team in possession faces first-and-10.

State Full tuple How we get there
sleads_{\mathrm{lead}} (Opponent,1st,10,Own 25,+1,1)(\text{Opponent},1\text{st},10,\text{Own 25},+1,1) The 57-yard field goal is good. The model assumes the ensuing kickoff leaves the opponent at its own 25.
strails_{\mathrm{trail}} (Opponent,1st,10,Own 40,−2,1)(\text{Opponent},1\text{st},10,\text{Own 40},-2,1) The opponent can kneel after a failed conversion, a missed kick, or a punt.
s1sts_{\mathrm{1st}} (Us,1st,10,Opp. 35,−2,1)(\text{Us},1\text{st},10,\text{Opp. 35},-2,1) We go for it and gain five yards.

strails_{\mathrm{trail}} uses the opponent’s own 40 as the representative spot after a failed attempt with no gain. A miss or punt can leave a different spot. We group those outcomes because the opponent kneels and wins in each one under the model.

We need two yards to convert, but the model assumes a five-yard gain on a successful attempt. That moves the ball from the opponent’s 40 to the 35 and leaves a 52-yard kick for the final snap (35+10+735+10+7).

3 Step 1 (t=1→0t=1\to0). Optimize the final play

The terminal state space is 𝒮0={Win,Loss}\mathcal S_0=\{\mathrm{Win},\mathrm{Loss}\}, from our team’s perspective. Start with the column vector

𝒗0=[V0*(Win),V0*(Loss)]⊤=[1,0]⊤. \mathbf v_0=[V_0^*(\mathrm{Win}),\ V_0^*(\mathrm{Loss})]^\top=[1,\ 0]^\top.

The superscript ⊤\top denotes transpose and turns the listed row into a column. With immediate rewards zero and γ=1\gamma=1, apply the Bellman optimality equation to each state at t=1t=1. Multiply the probability of each terminal outcome by its payoff in 𝒗0\mathbf v_0. At an opponent state, use the opponent’s modeled call.

V1*(slead)=0.90(1)+0.10(0)=0.90,V1*(strail)=0.00(1)+1.00(0)=0.00,V1*(s1st)=max⁡{0.65(1)+0.35(0)⏟52-yard field goal,0.12(1)+0.88(0)⏟Hail Mary}=0.65. \begin{aligned} V_1^*(s_{\mathrm{lead}}) &= 0.90(1)+0.10(0)=0.90,\\ V_1^*(s_{\mathrm{trail}}) &= 0.00(1)+1.00(0)=0.00,\\ V_1^*(s_{\mathrm{1st}}) &=\max\{\underbrace{0.65(1)+0.35(0)}_{\text{52-yard field goal}},\ \underbrace{0.12(1)+0.88(0)}_{\text{Hail Mary}}\}=0.65. \end{aligned}

At sleads_{\mathrm{lead}}, the opponent’s Hail Mary wins 10% of the time. At strails_{\mathrm{trail}}, the opponent kneels and wins. At s1sts_{\mathrm{1st}}, we choose between a 52-yard field goal and a Hail Mary. The kick has the higher value, so π1*(s1st)=FG52\pi_1^*(s_{\mathrm{1st}})=\mathrm{FG}_{52}, where FG52\mathrm{FG}_{52} is the 52-yard field-goal attempt.

Store the values in the order lead, trail, first down. This is the input to Step 2.

𝒗1=[V1*(slead),V1*(strail),V1*(s1st)]⊤=[0.90,0.00,0.65]⊤. \mathbf v_1=[V_1^*(s_{\mathrm{lead}}),\ V_1^*(s_{\mathrm{trail}}),\ V_1^*(s_{\mathrm{1st}})]^\top=[0.90,\ 0.00,\ 0.65]^\top.

4 Step 2 (t=2→1t=2\to1). Optimize the fourth-down decision

At t=2t=2, the Bellman optimality equation weights the values in 𝒗1\mathbf v_1 by the transition probabilities of each opening call. The transition matrix 𝑷2\mathbf P_2 has calls in rows (FG57\mathrm{FG}_{57}, Go\mathrm{Go}, Punt\mathrm{Punt}) and successor states in columns (sleads_{\mathrm{lead}}, strails_{\mathrm{trail}}, s1sts_{\mathrm{1st}}). Here FG57\mathrm{FG}_{57} means attempt the 57-yard field goal.

𝒒2=[Q2*(s0,FG57)Q2*(s0,Go)Q2*(s0,Punt)]=𝑷2𝒗1=[0.400.600.000.000.450.550.001.000.00][0.900.000.65]=[0.36000.35750.0000]. \begin{aligned} \mathbf q_2 &= \begin{bmatrix} Q_2^*(s_0,\mathrm{FG}_{57})\\ Q_2^*(s_0,\mathrm{Go})\\ Q_2^*(s_0,\mathrm{Punt}) \end{bmatrix} =\mathbf P_2\mathbf v_1\\[0.35em] &= \begin{bmatrix} 0.40 & 0.60 & 0.00\\ 0.00 & 0.45 & 0.55\\ 0.00 & 1.00 & 0.00 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}0.90\\0.00\\0.65\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0.3600\\0.3575\\0.0000\end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned}

The largest entry gives V2*(s0)=0.3600V_2^*(s_0)=0.3600, so π2*(s0)=FG57\pi_2^*(s_0)=\mathrm{FG}_{57}. The 57-yard attempt leads going for it by 0.25 percentage points. To value going for it, we needed the best action at s1sts_{\mathrm{1st}} even though the optimal opening kick never reaches that state.

5 What does the continuation policy change?

A continuation policy π\pi specifies what we do on the final play at t=1t=1. Our only final-play choice is at s1sts_{\mathrm{1st}}, which we reach if we go for it and convert. We compare two plans for that state. One calls for the 52-yard field goal, the optimal final play in this model. The other commits to a Hail Mary.

5.1 How the plan changes the value of going for it

Let Q2π(s0,a)Q_2^\pi(s_0,a) be our chance of winning if we choose action aa on fourth down at s0s_0 and follow policy π\pi afterward. Let V1π(s)V_1^\pi(s) be our chance of winning from state ss with one play left under that policy. Going for it reaches s1sts_{\mathrm{1st}} with probability 0.550.55 and strails_{\mathrm{trail}} with probability 0.450.45. If we fail to convert, the opponent kneels and wins, so V1π(strail)=0V_1^\pi(s_{\mathrm{trail}})=0 under either policy.

Q2π(s0,Go)=0.55V1π(s1st)+0.45V1π(strail)=0.55V1π(s1st). Q_2^\pi(s_0,\mathrm{Go}) =0.55\,V_1^\pi(s_{\mathrm{1st}}) +0.45\,V_1^\pi(s_{\mathrm{trail}}) =0.55\,V_1^\pi(s_{\mathrm{1st}}).

Last call after converting V1π(s1st)V_1^\pi(s_{\mathrm{1st}}), win probability after conversion Q2π(s0,Go)Q_2^\pi(s_0,\mathrm{Go}), win probability before the attempt
52-yard field goal 65% 0.55(0.65)=35.75%0.55(0.65)=35.75\%
Hail Mary 12% 0.55(0.12)=6.60%0.55(0.12)=6.60\%

For example, the Hail Mary gives us a 12% chance of winning after we convert. From fourth down, we have to get the first down and then win on the Hail Mary, which gives a 6.60% chance overall.

5.2 When the plan changes the fourth-down call

Let p57p_{57} be the probability of making the 57-yard field goal on fourth down. If we make it, we lead and the opponent has the ball for the last play. We hold the lead with probability 0.900.90. If we miss, the opponent kneels and wins. Neither outcome reaches s1sts_{\mathrm{1st}}, so our plan after a conversion has no effect on the value of kicking now.

Q2π(s0,FG57)=p57(0.90)+(1−p57)(0)=0.90p57. Q_2^\pi(s_0,\mathrm{FG}_{57}) =p_{57}(0.90)+(1-p_{57})(0) =0.90\,p_{57}.

Use the reference PDF’s 40% make probability first, then try a hypothetical 39%. The conversion probability stays at 55% in all four cases. Punting gives a 0% chance of winning in this model.

57-yard make probability p57p_{57} Last call after converting Go now Kick now (57 yd) Best fourth-down call
40% 52-yard field goal 35.75% 36.00% Kick now
40% Hail Mary 6.60% 36.00% Kick now
39% 52-yard field goal 35.75% 35.10% Go now
39% Hail Mary 6.60% 35.10% Kick now

At 40%, kicking now gives 36.00%, above the value of going under either plan. At 39%, kicking now gives 35.10%, between the two values of going. Going is the best call with the 52-yard field goal as the follow-up. Kicking now is the best call with the Hail Mary as the follow-up.
